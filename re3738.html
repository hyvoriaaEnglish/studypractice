Reading 37 — Zebras: More Than Stripes

Zebras are among the most recognizable animals in the world due to their distinctive black-and-white stripes. Native to the grasslands and savannas of Africa, these herbivorous mammals belong to the same family as horses and donkeys. Although zebras may appear similar, each individual possesses a unique stripe pattern, much like a human fingerprint.

For many years, scientists debated the purpose of zebra stripes. Some researchers believed that the patterns helped zebras camouflage themselves from predators. Others suggested that the stripes played a role in social recognition, allowing members of a herd to identify one another. More recent studies have proposed that the stripes may help deter biting insects, such as horseflies, which are less likely to land on striped surfaces.

Zebras are highly social animals and typically live in groups for protection. By remaining together, they can more effectively detect predators and warn one another of danger. However, habitat loss and human activities have threatened some zebra populations. Consequently, conservation efforts are increasingly important to ensure the survival of these remarkable animals and the ecosystems in which they live.

The passage introduces zebra characteristics and focuses particularly on the possible functions of their distinctive stripes, including camouflage, social recognition, and deterring insects.

4. Which of the following can be inferred from the passage?

The passage presents several competing explanations for zebra stripes, including camouflage, social recognition, and insect deterrence. Therefore, scientists have not reached complete agreement on their purpose.

5. The word "deter" in paragraph 2 most nearly means:

"To deter" means to discourage or prevent someone or something from doing a particular action. Here, zebra stripes may discourage biting insects from landing on them.

Reading 38 — The Global Popularity of K-Dramas

Korean dramas, commonly known as K-dramas, have gained immense popularity far beyond South Korea in recent decades. Their success can be attributed to several factors, including compelling storylines, high production quality, and emotionally engaging characters. Unlike some television series that continue for many years, many K-dramas consist of a single season with a clearly defined ending. This format appeals to viewers who prefer concise narratives and are reluctant to commit to lengthy series. In addition, streaming platforms have made K-dramas more accessible to international audiences by providing subtitles in multiple languages.

Despite their widespread appeal, K-dramas have also faced criticism. Some observers argue that certain series rely heavily on familiar plot devices, such as love triangles or improbable coincidences. Others contend that K-dramas occasionally present unrealistic standards of beauty, wealth, or romance. Nevertheless, supporters maintain that these dramas resonate with audiences because they explore universal themes such as family, friendship, ambition, and personal growth. As a result, K-dramas have become an important component of the global spread of Korean popular culture.
