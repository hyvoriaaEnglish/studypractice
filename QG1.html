Question 1

a. The initiative initially faced resistance from residents who were concerned about the cost and potential disruption to their daily routines.

b. After several months, however, the number of households participating in the programme had increased substantially, resulting in a noticeable reduction in landfill waste.

c. In response, the municipal authorities launched an extensive recycling campaign aimed at encouraging households to separate recyclable materials from general waste.

d. To address these concerns, officials organised a series of public consultations and introduced financial incentives for participating households.

e. The rapid growth of the city's population had placed considerable pressure on its already overstretched waste-management system.

The paragraph begins with the problem (e), followed by the authorities' response (c). Resistance is then introduced (a), followed by measures to address it (d). Finally, the positive result is presented (b).

Question 2

a. As a result, several technology companies have begun investing heavily in developing more efficient battery systems and expanding charging infrastructure.

b. However, the widespread adoption of electric vehicles has been hindered by concerns over their relatively limited driving range and the scarcity of charging stations.

c. In recent years, electric vehicles have emerged as a promising alternative to conventional petrol-powered cars.

d. These developments are expected to make electric vehicles increasingly accessible and practical for consumers in the years ahead.

e. Their growing popularity can be attributed largely to increasing environmental awareness and advances in battery technology.

The paragraph introduces electric vehicles (c), explains their growing popularity (e), then presents a challenge (b). 'As a result' introduces companies' response to this challenge (a), followed by the expected future outcome (d).

Question 3

a. Rather than relying solely on traditional lectures, the programme incorporates interactive simulations, collaborative projects and real-world case studies.

b. This approach has not only improved students' engagement but has also enabled them to develop practical skills that are difficult to acquire through conventional classroom instruction.

c. Recognising the need to equip students with skills relevant to an increasingly competitive labour market, the university recently redesigned its curriculum.

d. The changes were initially met with scepticism by some faculty members, who questioned whether the new model could maintain academic rigour.

e. Nevertheless, after the first year of implementation, the results suggested that the revised curriculum had achieved its intended objectives.

The university's curriculum change is introduced first (c), followed by details of the new programme (a). The initial scepticism is then mentioned (d). 'Nevertheless' signals a positive result (e), which is further explained in (b).

Question 4

a. Consequently, researchers have turned their attention to developing alternative materials that can decompose naturally without releasing harmful substances.

b. One promising solution is biodegradable packaging, which is increasingly being adopted by businesses seeking to reduce their environmental footprint.

c. The widespread use of conventional plastic packaging has become a major environmental concern due to its persistence in ecosystems.

d. Although these alternatives are generally more expensive to produce, technological advances are gradually bringing down their cost.

e. If this trend continues, biodegradable materials could eventually become a viable replacement for traditional plastic in a wide range of industries.

The paragraph starts with the environmental problem caused by plastic (c). 'Consequently' introduces the researchers' response (a). A specific solution is then presented (b), followed by its current disadvantage and technological improvement (d), leading to the possible future outcome (e).

Question 5

a. The scheme was initially introduced on a small scale, with only a handful of participating businesses and local residents.

b. Encouraged by these results, the authorities subsequently expanded the programme to cover neighbouring districts.

c. The success of the initiative has since prompted other cities to consider implementing similar schemes of their own.

d. Within months, however, the initiative had generated considerable interest, as both businesses and consumers recognised its potential benefits.

e. In an effort to reduce traffic congestion and improve air quality, the city council introduced a comprehensive bicycle-sharing scheme.

The scheme is introduced first (e), followed by its initial small-scale implementation (a). 'Within months, however' shows the rapid growth in interest (d). 'Encouraged by these results' leads to expansion (b), and the final sentence presents the wider influence on other cities (c).

Question 6

d. However, advances in tunnelling technology allowed engineers to overcome several obstacles that had previously been considered insurmountable.

Question 7

d. The findings may have significant implications for conservation strategies, particularly in regions experiencing rapid environmental change.

e. Further research is now being conducted to determine whether similar adaptive mechanisms can be observed in other populations.

The paragraph begins with the researchers' long-held belief (b). 'However' introduces contradictory new evidence (c). 'This unexpected outcome' refers directly to that evidence (a). The implications of the findings are then discussed (d), followed by ongoing research (e).
