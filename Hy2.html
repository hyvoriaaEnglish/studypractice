What Advertisers Want From You

Advertisers use clever psychology to build long-term relationships with consumers. Their goal is to persuade you to feel a certain way about their brand using several powerful tools. First, they seek your trust. This is (46) __________ tech companies use minimalist designs and banks use the color blue to signal security. If you trust a brand, you are more likely to become a repeat customer. Next, they aim for an emotional connection. By featuring happy families or inspiring stories, brands like Coca-Cola associate their products (47) __________ happiness and love. They want you to buy their product to recapture those positive emotions. Finally, advertisers create a (48) __________ of urgency. Phrases like “Limited time only!” pressure consumers to make quick decisions to avoid (49) __________ out. By influencing your perception, advertisers build brand (50) __________ so that you will return to buy a product again and again. They aren’t just selling a product; they are selling an idea and a promise. Next time you see an advert, ask yourself: what feeling are they trying to sell me?

Question 46

This is ______ tech companies use minimalist designs and banks use the color blue to signal security.

Answer: WHY

Explanation: "This is why..." introduces the reason for something. The sentence explains why tech companies use minimalist designs and banks use blue.
Question 47

Brands like Coca-Cola associate their products ______ happiness and love.

Answer: WITH

Explanation: The fixed collocation is "associate A with B", meaning to connect one thing with another. Therefore: "associate their products with happiness and love."
Question 48

Finally, advertisers create a ______ of urgency.

Answer: SENSE

Explanation: The fixed expression is "create a sense of urgency", meaning to make people feel that they need to act immediately.
Question 49

Phrases like “Limited time only!” pressure consumers to make quick decisions to avoid ______ out.

Answer: MISSING

Explanation: The expression "miss out" means to fail to get or experience something. After "avoid", we use a gerund: avoid missing out.
Question 50

By influencing your perception, advertisers build brand ______ so that you will return to buy a product again and again.

Answer: LOYALTY

Explanation: Brand loyalty refers to the tendency of customers to repeatedly buy products from the same brand.

Memory and Technology

Although our memory has to cope with an enormous amount of information, we often criticise it for letting us down when we (51) __________ to recall names, passwords or appropriate quotations at crucial moments. We tend to assume that others are naturally more gifted, with above-average memories. In reality, however, everyone has similar potential, and the difference lies in how effectively individuals train and use their memory. The reason we are out of the habit is partly to do with technology. Today’s computers and digital devices serve as our external hard drives, (52) __________. Our phones keep our to-do lists and alert us of upcoming appointments, while our computers store any number of pertinent facts that can be retrieved at the touch of a key. Six hundred years ago, a trained memory was not just a handy tool; it was a pre-requisite for a cultured mind. Since books were thin on the ground, memorizing the (53) __________ contained in them was the only way to maintain access to it. It was not uncommon to find learned scholars (54) __________. Question 51
Although our memory has to cope with an enormous amount of information, we often criticise it for letting us down when we ______ to recall names, passwords or appropriate quotations.
A. struggle
B. manage
C. remember
D. promise
Correct answer: A. struggle

Explanation: "Struggle to do something" means to have difficulty doing something. The sentence means that we criticise our memory when we have difficulty recalling names, passwords, or quotations.
Question 52
Today’s computers and digital devices serve as our external hard drives, ______.
A. helping to increase the capacity of our brains
B. giving us access to limited online knowledge
C. storing useful information outside our brains
D. encouraging dependence on modern technology
Correct answer: C. storing useful information outside our brains

Explanation: The passage compares digital devices to external hard drives because they store information outside our brains. The following sentence supports this: phones store to-do lists and computers store facts. Therefore, C is the best answer.
Question 53
Since books were thin on the ground, memorizing the ______ contained in them was the only way to maintain access to it.
A. wisdom
B. detail
C. culture
D. message
Correct answer: A. wisdom

Explanation: The passage discusses learned scholars and the knowledge contained in books. "Wisdom contained in books" is the most natural and meaningful choice in this context. "Detail" would normally be plural here ("details"), while "culture" and "message" do not fit the context as well.
Question 54
It was not uncommon to find learned scholars ______.
A. whose expertise was only appreciated by the general public
B. whose intellectual achievements were widely admired across Europe
C. who could recite by heart whole tracts of religious or philosophical works
D. who depended largely on their relationship for their reputation
Correct answer: C. who could recite by heart whole tracts of religious or philosophical works

Explanation: The passage explains that scholars had highly trained memories because books were scarce. They could therefore memorize and recite large sections of religious or philosophical works. The phrase "recite by heart" means to say something from memory.
Question 55
They were able to do this because they read intensively rather than extensively, ______.
A. deliberately ignoring factual information
B. becoming distracted by digital media
C. putting value on quality over quantity
D. developing increasingly shorter attention spans
Correct answer: C. putting value on quality over quantity

Explanation: "Read intensively rather than extensively" means reading fewer materials but studying them deeply and carefully. Therefore, the idea is to value quality over quantity.
