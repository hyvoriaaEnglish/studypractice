📖 Economics: Opportunity Cost

In economics, opportunity cost (chi phí cơ hội) is one of the most fundamental principles driving human behavior and financial decision-making. At its core, opportunity cost represents the potential benefit that an individual, investor, or business misses out on when choosing one alternative over another. Because resources such as time, money, labor, and raw materials are finite, every choice to allocate resources toward a specific option inherently requires sacrificing the next best alternative.

To evaluate opportunity costs accurately, economists look at the return of the chosen option compared to the return of the best foregone option. Mathematically, the basic formula is expressed as:

$$\text{Opportunity Cost} = \text{FO} - \text{CO}$$

Where:
• FO (Foregone Option): The expected return or value of the best alternative option that was not chosen.
• CO (Chosen Option): The expected return or value of the selected option.

In production contexts, opportunity cost can also be expressed in terms of trade-offs between two outputs:

$$\text{Opportunity Cost of Good A} = \frac{\text{Loss in Good B}}{\text{Gain in Good A}}$$

Consider a practical financial example: suppose you have $10,000 to invest. You can choose Option A, investing in a corporate bond with a guaranteed annual return of 6% ($600/year), or Option B, investing in the stock market with an expected return of 9% ($900/year). If you decide to play it safe and select Option A, your explicit gain is $600. However, your opportunity cost is the $900 you could have earned minus the $600 you actually earned, resulting in a net foregone value of $300.

Beyond pure financial figures, opportunity cost plays a pivotal role in daily personal decisions. If a university student decides to spend two hours watching a movie instead of studying for an upcoming economics exam, the explicit cost is the ticket price. The opportunity cost, however, includes the two hours of study time lost and the potential drop in grade performance.

Understanding opportunity cost enables businesses to optimize capital allocation and helps individuals weigh trade-offs before making significant life decisions. While explicit costs appear directly on balance sheets, implicit opportunity costs remain invisible yet crucial for long-term efficiency.
