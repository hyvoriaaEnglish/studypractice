📊 Economics: Multiplier Effects

Score: 0 / 10

Reading Passage

In Keynesian macroeconomics, any change in fiscal policy—whether through government spending or taxation—causes a magnified impact on a nation's total output and aggregate demand. This phenomenon is known as the multiplier effect.

To understand how the multiplier works, one must first examine the Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC). The MPC measures the proportion of an aggregate raise in pay that a consumer spends on goods and services rather than saving it. Its counterpart, the Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS), satisfies the relationship:

$$\text{MPC} + \text{MPS} = 1$$

When the government directly changes its spending (\(G\)), the Expenditure Multiplier (\(M_E\)) dictates the final change in national income (\(Y\)):

$$M_E = \frac{1}{1 - \text{MPC}} = \frac{1}{\text{MPS}}$$

However, when the government changes taxation (\(T\)), the mechanism works indirectly. A tax change first alters disposable income (\(Y_d = Y - T\)). Because households save a fraction (\(\text{MPS}\)) of any change in disposable income, the initial shift in aggregate spending is smaller than the tax change itself. Consequently, the Tax Multiplier (\(M_T\)) is always smaller in magnitude than the spending multiplier and carries a negative sign:

$$M_T = -\frac{\text{MPC}}{1 - \text{MPC}} = -\frac{\text{MPC}}{\text{MPS}}$$

For example, assume an economy has an \(\text{MPC} = 0.75\) (\(\text{MPS} = 0.25\)).

  • The Expenditure Multiplier is \(\frac{1}{0.25} = 4\). If government spending increases by $60 billion, national income increases by \(\$60\text{ billion} \times 4 = +\$240\text{ billion}\).
  • The Tax Multiplier is \(-\frac{0.75}{0.25} = -3\). If government taxation increases by $60 billion, national income changes by \(\$60\text{ billion} \times (-3) = -\$180\text{ billion}\).

Notice that spending $60 billion expands the economy more than taxing $60 billion contracts it. If the government increases spending and taxes by the exact same amount simultaneously, the net economic impact is governed by the Balanced Budget Multiplier, which always equals exactly \(+1\).
